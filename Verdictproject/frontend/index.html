02 · Architecture

Two loops. One carries a guarantee—the other never pretends to.

The most important line in the product is the one between what Verdict promises and what it merely measures. It is drawn once, here, and never blurred.

◆ Loop A · Guaranteed Field level

Field extraction, with a distribution‑free coverage guarantee

Of the values it automates, Verdict can state in advance roughly how often it will be wrong — and that promise holds under .

  1. extractlocate each field with its verbatim span, page, and box
  2. scorecompute a distribution‑free
  3. calibrateset the threshold so ≤
  4. route / acceptautomate what’s covered, hand off the rest
  5. updateeach reviewer correction feeds the update

Guaranteed quantity is marginal miscoverage at α=0.10 — not conditional selective risk, which is reported separately and never conflated.

◇ Loop B · Instrumented Case level

Case decisions, instrumented but never guaranteed

The case verdict aggregates fields into an exposure. It runs no update and claims no coverage — labels arrive late and selectively.

  1. aggregatecompose reviewed fields into a case view
  2. derivecompute ratios — blocked on any unresolved input
  3. deciderisk tier and exposure, as telemetry
  4. measuretrack per segment, worst shown

Loop B does not inherit Loop A’s guarantee. Derived‑metric confidence is not propagated — an open problem, stated, not hidden.

03 · Intelligence

Verdict routes on the signal that actually wins— even when that means firing the calibrator.

A model that doesn’t earn its place on the routing path doesn’t get one. The check re‑decides automatically on every run.

06 · Methodology

Six papers, transcribed to source — and where they conflict, we say so.

Every gated formula was verified against the printed PDFs; paper‑vs‑synthesis conflicts are recorded, not buried. Read the full methodology →

  1. 01

    Geifman & El‑Yaniv · 2017

    Selective classification and the selection‑bias failure of max‑coverage thresholds — the reason the naive baseline breaks r* even i.i.d.

  2. 02

    Mozannar & Sontag · 2020

    Consistent estimators for learning to defer — and the warning that fairness does not compose end‑to‑end, which is why Loop B measures disparate impact.

  3. 03

    Gibbs & Candès · 2021

    — the online threshold update that keeps Loop A’s guarantee alive under distribution shift.

  4. 04

    Kamath, Jia & Liang · 2020

    Selective QA under domain shift — the calibrator design Verdict tests, and holds when it fails to beat softmax.

  5. 05

    Mohri & Hashimoto · 2024

    Conformal factuality guarantees for language models — the framing for attaching a distribution‑free promise to a generative extractor.

  6. 06

    Beneish · 1999

    Detection of earnings manipulation — the financial‑forensics signals behind contradiction checks (with the − conflict recorded in DISCREPANCIES.md).
